How we score

A plain-language breakdown of the GA score, lane verdicts, the FAIR/NOT FAIR call, and every chip you'll see on a player.

What is GA?

GA ("Game Advantage") is a 0–100 estimate of how strong a player looked before the game started — based only on information that existed at loading screen: their recent ranked history, their rank relative to the lobby, and whether they're on a champion and role they actually play.

The core philosophy: only what was knowable before the game counts. Nothing from the game itself (kills, damage, who actually won) feeds into GA — that would just be describing the result, not predicting it. GA is meant to answer "was this a winnable lobby?", not "who played well?".

GA components

Six components sum to the final GA (0–100, clamped):

ComponentMaxHow it's computed
Form27Last-5 ranked games win rate × 22, plus ±5 for a 3+ game streak (win or loss), minus 3 for session tilt, minus 2 for rust, minus 2 for AFK risk.
Performance25Average KDA over the last 5 games (each game's KDA capped at 10) × 5, capped at 25.
Champion comfort20Games on this exact champion in the last 5 × 4, plus a mastery-tier bonus (+8 at 100k+, +5 at 25k+, +2 at 5k+). Minus 5 if they're an OTP denied their signature champion this game.
Role heat153 + 12 × (share of their last 5 games spent in this game's role). 5/5 in role = 15, 0/5 = 3, no positional data at all = a neutral 8.
Rank vs lobby10Tiered by how their rank compares to the lobby average (10/7/5/3, unranked = 5). Overridden to 10 if they look like a smurf.
Season winrate5Their league-v4 season win rate, in 5 tiers (1–5). Needs 20+ season games to count for anything above a neutral 3.

"Rust" = their most recent ranked game was 14+ days ago. "Session tilt" = 3+ games in the ~3 hours before this one, with at least 2 losses. "Smurf" = account level under 60 with either a 58%+ season winrate (20+ games) or 4.0+ average KDA recently — bumps rank-vs-lobby straight to 10 regardless of displayed rank.

Lane verdicts

Each of the 5 lanes gets an adjusted GA per player before the two sides are compared:

adjusted GA = GA − 5 (if autofill) − 5 (if first-time on champ) − 8 (if countered by the enemy laner)

The gap between adjusted GAs decides the lane verdict:

GapVerdict
0–8EVEN
9–18favored
19+HEAVILY favored

This is why a lane can show a clear favorite even when the two players' raw GA numbers look close — an autofilled or off-champion player, or one walking into a known hard counter, is carrying risk the raw number doesn't show. The lane's tooltip explains it when this happens.

The FAIR / NOT FAIR call

The overall verdict is binary and reason-based: if any of the following are true, the lobby is called NOT FAIR (worded relative to your team — "against you" vs "in your favor" — though the verdict itself doesn't care which way it points):

  • Team GA gap of 12+ (average GA per team, including a duo-synergy bonus scaled by each proven duo's joint win record together, capped at 8 per team).
  • A tier spread inside one team of 2.5+ tiers.
  • An autofill/first-time risk asymmetry of 8+ between the two teams, against your team.
  • 2 or more lanes heavily favored to one side more than the other.
  • Any single lane a 30+ GA blowout.
  • A net lane advantage of 35+ GA summed across all 5 lanes (catches lopsided lanes that cancel out in the team average — e.g. two +30 lanes and one −14 still nets a huge swing the average alone would miss).
  • 2 or more first-time champions on your team.

Be honest with yourself about this

Every number on this page is a heuristic, not truth. GA is a rough pre-game estimate built from public ranked history — it has no idea about someone's actual mechanical skill, communication, or how they're feeling that day. A NOT FAIR lobby is absolutely winnable, and a FAIR one can still be a stomp. Treat the verdict as "was this a reasonable lobby on paper", not a guarantee or an excuse.

The champion counter matrix (lib/counters.mjs) is hand-authored from general matchup knowledge, not patch-tuned or exhaustive — it's deliberately conservative, and it's community-editable: if you think a matchup is wrong or missing, open a PR on GitHub.
